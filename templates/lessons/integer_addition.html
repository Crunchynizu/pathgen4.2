2-minute recap

Adding integers

Same signs: add the distances and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the distances and keep the sign of the number farther from zero.

Worked example

Compute -7 + 4.

  1. The signs are different, so subtract the distances: 7 - 4 = 3.
  2. -7 is farther from zero than 4, so the answer is negative.

-7 + 4 = -3

Addition

Zero pairs

When you add integers with different signs, a positive chip and a negative chip cancel. Each pair equals zero.

Example: -5 + 3. Start with 5 negative chips. Add 3 positive chips. Three pairs cancel and two negatives remain, so -5 + 3 = -2.

Same signs

Add the distances and keep the sign.

  • (-4) + (-3) = -7
  • 4 + 3 = 7

Different signs

Subtract the smaller distance from the larger one. The answer takes the sign of the number farther from zero: -9 + 4 = -5, and 9 + (-4) = 5.

Common mistake

-6 + 2 = -8 is wrong. The signs are different, so subtract the distances: 6 - 2 = 4, and keep the negative sign. Correct: -6 + 2 = -4.

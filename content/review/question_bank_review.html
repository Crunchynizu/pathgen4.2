Practice questions: please check before students see them

These are the practice questions for the six PathGen lessons: 118 in all. Students answer 4 or 5 per lesson, picked by the system from these pools. Most come from PathGen 3.0; 13 are new.

Please check, for each question:

  1. The answer marked ✓ is correct, and only one choice is correct.
  2. The skill fits. Lesson 1 now has four skills (number line, opposites, absolute value, compare and order), and adding and subtracting are separate skills.
  3. The level (Easy, Medium or Hard) fits Grade 7. The system uses it to give an easier or harder next question.
  4. The hint helps a student who got it wrong, without giving the answer away. Students see it only after a wrong first answer, before their one retry. 86 of the hints are new; the rest come from 3.0.
  5. The wording is clear. 18 questions used to have a picture, but 3.0's pictures were blank, so they are text only now; 4 of them were reworded to put the numbers in the text.

Tick "OK" or write the change. The pretest and posttest are not here: they stay exactly as approved. At the end are the 10 short feedback texts students see after practice.

Feedback after practice

After each practice, the student sees their score, then "Next focus: (skill)" with one of these short texts. Please check them like the questions.

Number line · Integers on the number line

Integers are whole numbers, their negatives, and zero. On a number line, zero is in the middle, positive integers go right and negative integers go left.

Example: A point 4 units to the left of 0 is at -4.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Opposites · Opposites

Opposites are the same distance from zero, on different sides. Keep the number and change its sign.

Example: The opposite of 9 is -9, and the opposite of -2 is 2. Zero is its own opposite.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Absolute value · Absolute value is a distance

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero, so it is never negative.

Example: |-6| = 6 and |6| = 6, because both are 6 units from zero.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Compare and order · Compare by position

On a number line, numbers get greater to the right. A number farther left is less, even when its digits look bigger.

Example: -8 < -3, because -8 is farther left than -3.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Addition · Adding integers

Same signs: add the distances and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the distances and keep the sign of the number farther from zero.

Example: -9 + 4: 9 - 4 = 5, and -9 is farther from zero, so -9 + 4 = -5.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Subtraction · Adding the opposite

To subtract an integer, add its opposite.

Example: 3 - (-5) = 3 + 5 = 8, and 2 - 6 = 2 + (-6) = -4.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Multiplication · The sign of a product

Multiply the distances first, then decide the sign: same signs give a positive product, different signs give a negative product.

Example: (-3) x 7: 3 x 7 = 21, and the signs are different, so the product is -21.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Division · The sign of a quotient

Divide the distances first, then use the same sign rules as multiplication.

Example: (-35) / (-5): 35 / 5 = 7, and the signs are the same, so the quotient is 7.

☐ OK as is   ☐ Change: ____________________________________________

Expressions · Order of operations

Work inside grouping symbols first, then multiply and divide from left to right, then add and subtract from left to right.

Example: 4 + 2 x (-3) = 4 + (-6) = -2. Multiply before you add.

Signed quantities · Signed changes

Rise, gain and deposit are positive changes. Fall, loss and withdrawal are negative changes. Add the change to the starting value.

Example: A diver at -5 m rises 3 m: -5 + 3 = -2, so the diver is 2 m below the surface.
